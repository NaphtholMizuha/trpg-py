The current engine supports only these step types and kinds:
- select.target
- select.area
- select.filtered
- check.attack
- check.save
- check.ability
- check.skill
- damage.apply
- heal.apply
- resource.consume
- effect.add
- effect.remove
- state.set
- state.adjust

Primitive responsibilities:
- `select.target` chooses a known target or target list when targeting is already determined.
- `select.area` determines which creatures are covered by an origin, shape, and targeting rule.
- `select.filtered` narrows an entity pool by side, tags, alive state, or other filters.
- `check.attack` produces an attack-roll outcome that later consequence steps may consume.
- `check.save` produces per-target save outcomes that later consequence steps may consume.
- `check.ability` and `check.skill` produce non-attack, non-save roll outcomes.
- `damage.apply` converts damage formulas plus optional save or attack outcomes into HP writes.
- `heal.apply` converts healing intent into HP increases and caps when max HP is available.
- `resource.consume` spends bounded resources such as spell slots.
- `effect.add` and `effect.remove` update effect lists.
- `state.set` and `state.adjust` handle the remaining direct state changes that are not already better expressed by damage, heal, resource, or effect.

Think in dataflow, not only in task families:
- selection steps determine who is affected
- check steps produce roll outcomes
- consequence steps such as damage, heal, resource, and effect write the main results
- state steps are the fallback for residual direct updates

Freehand composition is allowed when it stays inside the supported DSL vocabulary, matches the TaskDraft, and survives template/lint checks.
Prefer domain primitives over `state.set` or `state.adjust` when a dedicated primitive already exists.

Hard rules:
- Do not invent project-external step types such as `read`, `calculate`, `invoke`, `conditional`, or `write`.
- Do not invent project-external kinds such as `ReadProperties`, `DetermineSpellUse`, `DetermineTargetsInArea`, `DexteritySavingThrow`, `ApplyFireballDamage`, `UpdateHP`, or `ConsumeSpellSlot`.
- Use only the namespaces `context`, `state`, and `result` inside `$ref`.
- When a judgment says the task needs targeting or area coverage, lower it to `select.target` or `select.area`.
- When a judgment says a creature makes an attack or save roll, lower it to `check.attack` or `check.save`.
- When a judgment says HP changes because of damage, prefer `damage.apply` over a custom business step.
- When a write consumes a spell slot or other resource path, prefer `resource.consume`.
- Use `state.set` or `state.adjust` only for direct state updates that are not already covered by `damage`, `heal`, `resource`, or `effect`.
- Reuse earlier `result.*` outputs instead of recomputing the same game fact in later steps.

Useful minimum constraints:
- `select.area` requires `shape` and `origin`
- `check.attack` requires `dice`
- `check.save` requires `dice`, `ability`, and `dc` or `dc_path`
- `damage.apply` requires `targets` and `damage`
- `resource.consume` requires `path`
- `state.set` and `state.adjust` require `path`
